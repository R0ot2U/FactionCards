About Informed Crusader

Informed Crusader collects and analyses competitive Warhammer 40,000 tournament data from Best Coast Pairings (BCP) and New Recruit events. It provides win rates, play rates, detachment breakdowns, top player rankings, matchup data, player ELO ratings, and weekly meta trends — all from real tournament results.

Data is refreshed periodically after tournament weekends. Time windows are anchored to the most recent event date, not today's calendar date, ensuring you always see complete tournament data rather than empty windows.

Metric Definitions

Play Rate %

The percentage of all players in the window using this faction. A faction with a 10% play rate means 10 out of every 100 players used it.

Win Rate %

Win rate across all games in the window. A draw counts as 0.5 wins. Calculated from the perspective of the faction's player, not their opponent.

50% is even. Above 55% is strong; below 45% is weak. Colour-coded: green ≥ 55%, yellow 45–55%, red ≤ 45%.

Meta Health

Statistical assessment of faction strength based on Wilson 95% confidence interval (CI) lower bound. Uses a pessimistic estimate to avoid over-rating factions with small samples.

5 tiers: Strongly Over (CI > 55%), Slightly Over (CI > 50%), Neutral (50%), Slightly Under (CI < 50%), Strongly Under (CI < 45%). A 51.5% win rate can show "underperform" if the CI lower bound is below 50%.

Trend (Factions)

Win-rate change compared to the previous window of the same length. For example, with a 30-day window, Trend compares the most recent 30 days to the 30 days before that. Shows whether the faction's performance is improving or declining.

▲ means improving, ▼ means declining. Shown as percentage points (e.g., +3.2%). A small change (±0.5%) is considered flat (shown as —).

Trend (Players)

ELO rating change within the current window. Calculated as rating after the last game minus rating before the first game in the window. Shows whether a player's skill rating rose or fell.

Measured in rating points (e.g., +258.8), not percentage. ▲ means rating increased, ▼ means decreased. A small change (±0.5 points) is considered flat (shown as —).

X-0 Rate

Percentage of players who went undefeated with this faction (0 losses, 0 draws, at least 1 win). Measures ceiling potential — how often does the faction take players to perfect records?

X-1 Rate

Percentage of players who dropped exactly one game — either 1 loss and 0 draws, or 0 losses and 1 draw. Combined with X-0, this shows how many players finished with strong records (top tables).

Tournament Wins

Number of 1st place finishes (lists with placing = 1). Direct measure of championship performance. Shown both faction-wide and per detachment.

Top Detachment

The detachment used by the most players for this faction in the window. Not necessarily the strongest — just the most popular.

Top Disposition

The most common Force Disposition for this faction (11th Edition only). Five options: Purge the Foe, Take and Hold, Priority Assets, Disruption, Reconnaissance. Choice determines available secondary objectives.

Force Disposition

An 11th Edition list construction choice. Determines which secondary objectives are available during games. Shown on faction detail pages when 11th edition data is selected.

Players

Total number of players recorded for this faction or detachment in the window. Each player at each event contributes one entry.

Games

Total number of individual game results recorded. Each round of an event produces one game per player.

Lists

Total number of army lists submitted. In the dispositions context, this counts lists that declared this Force Disposition. Same as Players count in most contexts.

Avg VP

Average (mean) victory points scored per game. Shows typical scoring output for a disposition or matchup. Higher values indicate more aggressive or higher-scoring mission types.

Used on disposition detail pages and previously in matchup analysis. Purge the Foe typically scores highest (~44 VP average), while Disruption and Reconnaissance score lower (~18-19 VP average).

Dispositions Overview

The Dispositions page (11th Edition only) shows performance across the five canonical Force Dispositions: Purge the Foe, Take and Hold, Priority Assets, Disruption, and Reconnaissance. Force Dispositions determine which secondary objectives are available during games.

Force Disposition Win Rate

Overall win rate for all lists using each disposition. Shows which dispositions are performing best across the meta. Horizontal bar chart, sorted by play rate (most popular at top).

Matchup Heatmap

5×5 grid showing win rate when one disposition (row) plays against another (column). Mirror matches (same disposition on both sides) are excluded and shown as grey cells.

Read as: "When [row disposition] plays into [column disposition], the row's win rate is X%". Colour scale: red (poor matchup) → yellow (even) → green (favourable).

Matchup Breakdown

Expandable tables showing detailed head-to-head records for each disposition against the other four. Click a disposition name to expand and see games played and win rate vs each opponent.

Top 5 Armies / Detachments

Shows the best-performing armies or detachments for each disposition. Sortable by Lists (most popular), Games (most played), Win % (best performance, default), or X-0 % (undefeated rate).

When you change the sort, the displayed top 5 changes to show the actual top 5 for that metric, not just re-sorted results. For example, sorting by Win % shows the 5 highest-performing armies, even if they're not the most popular.

Disposition Detail Page

Each disposition has a dedicated detail page showing full breakdowns, accessible by clicking a disposition name from the overview page or matchup tables.

Hero Stats

Six key metrics displayed at the top: Lists (count), Games (count), Win Rate (%), Avg VP (victory points), X-0 Rate (undefeated %), X-1 Rate (one-loss %).

Faction Breakdown

Full table of all factions using this disposition, with list count, games played, win rate, and X-0 rate. Shows which factions prefer this disposition and how they perform with it.

Detachment Breakdown

All detachment combinations (base_archetype format) using this disposition. Includes multi-detachment combos like "Drukhari — Exhibition Of Slaughter + Skysplinter Assault". Shows lists, games, win rate, X-0 rate.

Matchup Table

Head-to-head records against the other four dispositions (mirrors excluded). Shows games played and win rate for each pairing. Sortable by any column.

Top Players

Top 10 players by win rate using this disposition in the current window. Minimum 3 games required. Shows player name, faction, wins-losses-draws record, and win rate.

Faction Detail Page

Army Performance vs Meta

Stat grid showing: Win Rate, Representation %, X-0 Rate, X-1 Rate, and Tournament Wins. Gives a quick snapshot of how the faction performs against the field.

Faction Rankings

Detachment Breakdown

Players, win rate, and tournament wins split by detachment (formation). Play % here is relative to this faction's own players, not all players. Includes a pie chart with switcher for Play %, Win %, and Tournament Wins views.

Force Dispositions

Table showing disposition distribution and win rates for this faction (11th Edition only). Shows which Force Disposition players choose most often and how each performs. Includes pie chart visualization.

Win Rate into Opponent

This faction's win rate against each specific opponent faction. Only matchups with at least 5 games are shown. Sorted best to worst.

Top Players

Players ranked by win rate with this faction in the window. Minimum 3 games required to appear. A "View List" link (where available) opens the original BCP or New Recruit list.

Players per Week / Win Rate per Week

Weekly breakdown of player count and win rate. Shows participation trends and whether performance has shifted recently. Each point is one calendar week.

Weekly Performance Table

Sortable table view of the timeline data: Week, Players, Rep %, Win %, Games. Placed below the timeline charts. Allows sorting by any column to identify peak performance weeks or participation spikes.

Player Rankings

ELO Rating

Skill rating based on game results and opponent strength. All players start at 1500. Higher rating means stronger competitive performance.

Beating a higher-rated opponent increases your rating more than beating a lower-rated opponent. Losing to a lower-rated opponent decreases your rating more than losing to a higher-rated opponent.

Peak Rating

Highest ELO rating this player has ever achieved across all events in the database. Shows their best historical performance.

K-Factor

How volatile the player's rating is. Determines how much their rating changes after each game.

40 = new player (fewer than 30 games), 32 = standard (30+ games), 24 = experienced (100+ games). Higher K means bigger rating swings per game.

Consistency

Score from 0 to 1 measuring how stable a player's performance is across events. Higher = more consistent results.

Calculated from variance in win rates across multiple events. A player who consistently goes 3-2 has higher consistency than a player who alternates between 5-0 and 1-4.

Upset Rate

Win rate specifically against higher-rated opponents. Measures a player's ability to punch above their weight class.

Only counts games where the opponent's rating was higher than the player's rating at the time of the match. A 40% upset rate means winning 2 out of every 5 games against stronger opponents.

Trend

Rating change over the current window. See "Trend (Players)" in Metric Definitions above for details.

Tournament Map

Interactive Map

Shows tournament locations worldwide. Appears on the Factions and Tournaments pages. Dot size reflects the number of tournaments and total players at that location.

Multiple tournaments in the same city are grouped together. You can zoom and pan to explore different regions.

Map Tooltips

Hover over any dot to see: location name, number of tournaments, total players, and top 3 factions by X-0 rate (undefeated percentage).

If no faction has undefeated players at that location, the tooltip shows top 3 by X-1 rate instead. This highlights which factions perform best at each location.

Statistical Methodology

Wilson Confidence Interval

Meta Health uses the Wilson 95% confidence interval (CI) instead of raw win rate percentages. The CI gives a range estimate for the "true" win rate, accounting for sample size.

A faction with 51.5% win rate but only 20 games might have a CI of 45%–58%, meaning the true win rate could plausibly be anywhere in that range. We use the lower bound (45%) for a pessimistic, conservative assessment.

Why CI Lower Bound?

Using the CI lower bound prevents over-rating factions with lucky small samples. A faction with 60% win rate over 10 games has a wide CI (e.g., 47%–73%), so the lower bound keeps the health rating cautious until more data confirms the performance.

Incomplete Data Filtering

Players with no game records (wins + losses + draws = 0) are excluded from X-0/X-1/Tournament Wins calculations. These represent unparsed or incomplete data, often from events where list scraping failed.

Data Sources & Caveats

  • Sources: Best Coast Pairings (BCP) and New Recruit tournament platforms. Data covers GT-level and above events in the window.
  • Army list parsing: Player lists are parsed from BCP and New Recruit export formats. Faction and detachment are extracted from the list text, not the BCP roster (which can be inaccurate for some factions).
  • Sample sizes & filters: Win rates for factions or detachments with small samples (< 20 games) should be treated with caution. The following minimum thresholds are applied:
    • Factions require min 5 games to appear in the main faction table
    • Detachment stats require min 5 games
    • Matchup data requires min 5 games per pairing
    • Player rankings require min 3 games (for 7d/14d/30d windows) or min 5 games (for 60d window)
    • Trend arrows require min 10 games in the prior window (shows "—" if insufficient)
  • Team events: Team pairing dynamics differ from solo play. Win rates in Teams mode reflect performance within the team pairing context.
  • Refresh cadence: Data is rebuilt periodically after new events are processed. The build date in the footer shows when the current data was generated.
  • Time window anchoring: Windows (7d, 14d, 30d, 60d) are anchored to the most recent event date in the dataset, not today's calendar date. This ensures windows always show complete tournament data rather than empty periods. For example, if the last data refresh was Tuesday May 20 after a weekend of events, the "7 days" window shows May 13–20 (the week of tournament data), not May 17–24 (which would be empty).
